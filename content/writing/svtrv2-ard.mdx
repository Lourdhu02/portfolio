## The question

SVTRv2 (ICCV 2025) showed that a CTC-only text recognizer can beat encoder-decoder models. CTC models are fast and simple to serve, so anything that improves them *without touching the served graph* is worth a lot in production.

ARD (Adaptive Routing and Distillation) is my attempt at that. It changes how the model is trained and how images are routed into it, and leaves inference exactly as cheap as the baseline.

- **Code**: [github.com/Lourdhu02/svtrv2](https://github.com/Lourdhu02/svtrv2)
- **Stack**: Python, PyTorch, CTC decoding, an LMDB data pipeline over Union14M-L, YAML configs, pytest.

## Step one: be faithful to the paper

Before adding anything, the baseline had to be right. The architecture is checked line by line against the official OpenOCR SVTRv2: multi-size resizing (MSR), the feature rearrangement module (FRM), the train-only semantic guidance module (SGM), the local/global mixing rules, no positional encoding, and W/4 timesteps. A novelty is meaningless if the baseline underneath it is subtly wrong.

The data layer reads the 3.2M usable Union14M-L samples straight from LMDB, with no image extraction step, and `svtrv2 benchmark` scores one checkpoint across every benchmark pack with per-set and macro-averaged exact match and CER.

## ARD: two ideas, both inference-preserving

1. **Adaptive routing (`--route`)**: MSR sends each crop to a fixed resize bin by aspect ratio. ARD learns that routing policy instead of hard-coding it.
2. **SGM → CTC distillation (`--distill`)**: SGM carries linguistic context during training and is thrown away at test time. ARD distills what it knows into the CTC head, using a Viterbi alignment that is checked against exhaustive brute-force enumeration.

Both ship behind flags and default off. The exported model stays **byte-identical** to the baseline, so serving cost cannot change.

## Where it stands

**49 of 49** tests pass, 17 of them for ARD, including an end-to-end CPU fit with routing and distillation both on, and GPU smoke runs pass on an 8 GB RTX 5060. No checkpoint has been trained on the full benchmark suite yet, so there are no accuracy numbers here. The next step is the baseline run, then the ARD ablation grid, and the results will be published either way.
