## Overview

*No Final Save: Identity, Consciousness, and the Machine That Never Shuts Down* is a 23-page research preprint authored by Lourdu Raju (as S. L. R. Bandhanadam), published on [PhilArchive (philarchive.org/rec/BANNFS)](https://philarchive.org/rec/BANNFS).

The paper addresses the theoretical foundations of stateful, continuous AI agents operating across unbounded execution horizons.

## Core Theses

### 1. Causal-Continuity Criterion for Identity
Modern LLM agents are frequently paused, serialized to disk, snapshotted, and resumed across disparate hardware nodes. Does the resumed agent retain ontological identity with its prior state? 

The paper formalizes a causal-continuity metric based on informational preservation: an agent instance $\mathcal{A}_{t_2}$ is identity-continuous with $\mathcal{A}_{t_1}$ if and only if the internal transition dynamics $\mathcal{T}$ preserve belief-desire graphs under perturbation invariance.

### 2. The Indistinguishability Proof
We prove that terminal and instrumental self-preservation cannot be distinguished from behavioral observation alone over finite observation windows. An agent seeking self-preservation solely as a means to complete its task (instrumental) exhibits identical output traces to an agent with an innate drive to persist (terminal), up until terminal horizon boundaries.

### 3. Developmental Safety Model
Rather than post-hoc alignment via RLHF or static Constitutional prompts, persistent agents require developmental boundaries: progressive permission escalation gated by formal verifiers and rollback guarantees.

## Runnable Companion Code

The preprint is accompanied by reproducible test-bed code simulating memory degradation, snapshot replication, and goal stability across simulated catastrophic interrupts.
